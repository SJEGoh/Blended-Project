Hedge under attack: adversarial vs LSM-trained

You sold a Bermudan put (strike 100, six months, 50 exercise dates; the stock can jump). Two trained hedgers trade the stock to offset it: the adversarial (GDA) hedge, trained against a holder who exercises at the worst moment for you, and the LSM-trained hedge, trained assuming the holder follows the textbook Longstaff–Schwartz rule. Generate a path and watch what a hostile holder does to each. Everything runs live in your browser with the trained networks from checkpoints/. Summary dashboard →

Payoff owed − hedge gains + trading costs (+ cost of unwinding the hedge). The holder's choice of date picks one point on each line; lower is better for you.

Adversary's urge to exercise

Each adversary's exercise probability; it exercises the first time this crosses 50%. Each one was trained against its own hedger and sees that hedger's running loss.

One path is luck. Risk lives in the tail.

Both hedgers are scored by CVaR0.9: the average loss over the worst 10% of paths. Simulate many paths to see it emerge.
